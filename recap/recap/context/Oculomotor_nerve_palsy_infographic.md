Oculomotor nerve palsy occurs when the oculomotor nerve (CN III) is compressed or receives inhibited blood flow and is damaged. Because CN III controls voluntary movement in the eye and the parasympathetic action of the pupils, CN III palsy can cause double vision, droopy eyelids, and blown pupils.

Symptoms of oculomotor nerve palsy: a down and out eye. A drooping eyelid. A blown pupil. Double vision.

Causes of oculomotor nerve palsy: pressure on the nerve damages the outer fibers. Reduced blood supply to the nerve damages the inner fibers.

The two primary causes of CN III palsy are increased pressure on the nerve and reduced blood supply to the nerve. Increased pressure is typically caused by aneurysms, tumours, or herniation and damages the outer nerve fibers, causing symptoms like blown pupils and drooping eyelids. Loss of blood flow typically results from conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure. It tends to damage the inner nerve fibers, which causes symptoms affecting voluntary eye muscle movement such as a down and out eye and double vision.

The oculomotor nerve leaves the brain stem and supplies the levator palpebrae superioris, the superior rectus muscle, the medial rectus muscle, the inferior rectus muscle, the inferior oblique muscle, the ciliary muscle and the sphincter pupillae.

The oculomotor nerve (CN III) is responsible for eye movement, eyelid elevation, pupil constriction, and focusing.

The oculomotor nerve has two types of motor fibers: voluntary inner nerve fibers, and involuntary outer nerve fibers.

The oculomotor nerve controls the medial rectus muscle, the inferior rectus muscle, the inferior oblique muscle, and the levator muscle. These muscles control voluntary eye movement.

The oculomotor nerve controls the sphincter pupillae and the ciliary muscle, which are responsible for involuntary movement.
